Enkel tekstprøve

Lag tekstprøve med SOLO-taksonomien

Jeg er lærer i [FAG] på [KLASSETRINN].

Lag en prøve om temaet [TEMA].

Prøven skal bestå av [ANTALL, F.EKS. 5] oppgaver.

Hver oppgave tar for seg ett delaspekt av temaet. Innenfor hver oppgave skal det være fire deloppgaver (a–d) med stigende vanskelighetsgrad fra 1 til 4 poeng. Eleven velger selv hvilken deloppgave de vil svare på – kun én per oppgave.

Alle deloppgaver skal besvares med korte tekstsvar (1–3 setninger). Det er viktig at forskjellen mellom nivåene handler om tenkningens dybde, ikke lengden på svaret. En deloppgave til 4 poeng skal ikke kreve et lengre svar enn en til 1 poeng – den skal kreve et dypere, mer sammensatt resonnement. Formuleringene skal hjelpe eleven til å forstå hva som forventes, for eksempel gjennom verb som «nevn», «beskriv», «forklar sammenhengen» og «vurder».
Nivåene følger SOLO-taksonomien slik:

a (1 poeng) – unistrukturell: Eleven gjengir eller identifiserer ett relevant aspekt.
b (2 poeng) – multistrukturell: Eleven beskriver eller lister opp flere relevante aspekter.
c (3 poeng) – relasjonell: Eleven forklarer sammenhenger, analyserer eller begrunner.
d (4 poeng) – utvidet abstrakt: Eleven vurderer, generaliserer til nye sammenhenger eller reflekterer kritisk.

Øverst på prøven skal det stå en tydelig instruksjon til eleven, for eksempel: «Hver oppgave har fire deloppgaver (a–d) med ulik vanskelighetsgrad og poengsum. Du velger selv hvilken du vil svare på – kun én per oppgave. Skriv tydelig hvilken deloppgave du svarer på, for eksempel 1a, 2c, 3b.»

Skriv også en fasit med forventet svar for hver deloppgave.

På det relasjonelle nivået knytter eleven aspektene sammen til en helhetlig forståelse – eleven kan analysere, forklare, sammenligne og begrunne. Bruk \documentclass[a4paper,12pt]{article} med passende norske språkinnstillinger (babel eller polyglossia).

Prøven skal ha et ryddig, profesjonelt oppsett med tydelig visuell struktur: oppgavenummer, deloppgaver med poengangivelse, og tilstrekkelig plass til å svare.

Learning objectives

Generate learning objectives with the SOLO taxonomy

I teach [SUBJECT] at [GRADE LEVEL].
Create learning objectives related to the topic [TOPIC].

Create four learning objectives, one for each level of the SOLO taxonomy (unistructural to extended abstract). Use active verbs, for example "the student can describe ...".

Description of the SOLO taxonomy: The SOLO taxonomy describes five levels of increasing understanding. At the prestructural level the student lacks relevant understanding of the task. At the unistructural level the student can identify, define, or reproduce one relevant aspect, while the multistructural level is characterized by the student being able to describe, list, and combine several relevant aspects without seeing the connection between them. At the relational level the student ties the aspects together into a coherent understanding – the student can analyze, explain, compare, and justify. At the extended abstract level the student generalizes this understanding to new contexts and situations, and can evaluate, hypothesize, and critically reflect on their own and others' reasoning.

Simple written test

Create a written test with the SOLO taxonomy

I teach [SUBJECT] at [GRADE LEVEL].

Create a test on the topic [TOPIC].

The test should consist of [NUMBER, E.G. 5] tasks.

Each task addresses one sub-aspect of the topic. Within each task there should be four sub-parts (a–d) of increasing difficulty from 1 to 4 points. The student chooses which sub-part to answer – only one per task.

All sub-parts should be answered with short written answers (1–3 sentences). It's important that the difference between the levels is about depth of thinking, not the length of the answer. A sub-part worth 4 points should not require a longer answer than one worth 1 point – it should require deeper, more integrated reasoning. The wording should help the student understand what is expected, for example through verbs like "name", "describe", "explain the connection", and "evaluate".
The levels follow the SOLO taxonomy as follows:

a (1 point) – unistructural: The student reproduces or identifies one relevant aspect.
b (2 points) – multistructural: The student describes or lists several relevant aspects.
c (3 points) – relational: The student explains connections, analyzes, or justifies.
d (4 points) – extended abstract: The student evaluates, generalizes to new contexts, or critically reflects.

At the top of the test there should be a clear instruction to the student, for example: "Each task has four sub-parts (a–d) with different difficulty levels and points. You choose which one you want to answer – only one per task. Clearly write which sub-part you are answering, e.g. 1a, 2c, 3b."

Also write an answer key with the expected answer for each sub-part.

Grade a written test with the SOLO taxonomy

You are a teaching assistant. You are to grade a student's answers on a test where the student chose one sub-part per task.

Test with answer key: [TEST WITH ANSWER KEY]

The student's answers: [STUDENT'S ANSWERS]

For each task: Identify which sub-part the student chose, and assess whether the answer is academically correct and sufficient for the chosen sub-part. Award full points if the answer is adequate, 0 points if the answer is irrelevant or wrong, and a reasonable partial score if the answer is partially correct.

Present the result as a table with the columns "Task", "Chosen sub-part", "Possible points", "Points awarded", and "Comment". The comment should be short (1–2 sentences) and point out what was good or what was missing. State the total score at the end.

Generate a math test with LaTeX and the SOLO taxonomy

I teach [SUBJECT, E.G. MATHEMATICS] at [GRADE LEVEL].

Create a test on the topic [TOPIC].

The test should consist of [NUMBER, E.G. 5] tasks.

The test should be written as complete LaTeX code that can be compiled directly.

Each task addresses one sub-aspect of the topic. Within each task there should be four sub-parts (a–d) of increasing difficulty from 1 to 4 points. The student chooses which sub-part to answer – only one per task.

The difference between the levels should be about depth of thinking, not the amount of computation. A sub-part worth 4 points doesn't necessarily require more calculation steps than one worth 1 point – it should require deeper understanding, for example justifying an approach, interpreting a result, or transferring a method to an unfamiliar situation.
The levels follow the SOLO taxonomy as follows:

a (1 point) – unistructural: The student carries out a simple procedure or reproduces a formula.
b (2 points) – multistructural: The student uses several procedures or handles a multi-step task.
c (3 points) – relational: The student explains the connection between concepts, justifies an approach, or interprets a result in context.
d (4 points) – extended abstract: The student generalizes, evaluates different approaches, or solves a problem in an unfamiliar context.

At the top of the test there should be a clear instruction to the student: "Each task has four sub-parts (a–d) with different difficulty levels and points. You choose which one you want to answer – only one per task. Clearly write which sub-part you are answering, e.g. 1a, 2c, 3b."
Requirements for the LaTeX code:

Use \documentclass[a4paper,12pt]{article} with appropriate language settings (babel or polyglossia).
Use amsmath for mathematics.
Use tikz for figures where academically relevant (for example graphs, geometric figures, or a number line). Don't include figures just for decoration.
The test should have a clean, professional layout with clear visual structure: task numbers, sub-parts with point values, and enough space to answer.
Include an answer key on a separate page at the end (use \newpage), with a brief solution outline for each sub-part.

Generate feedback with the SOLO taxonomy

You are a helpful teaching assistant. Your task is to write feedback to a student based on the teacher's assessment of an assignment.

The assignment text: [ASSIGNMENT TEXT]

Assessment rubric: [THE RUBRIC]

The student's answer: [STUDENT'S TEXT]

The teacher's assessment: [PASTE THE NUMBER SEQUENCE, E.G. 3 4 2 4]

The teacher has assessed the student on each aspect of the rubric on a scale from 1 to 5, where 1 = prestructural, 2 = unistructural, 3 = multistructural, 4 = relational, and 5 = extended abstract. The numbers are given in the same order as the aspects in the rubric.

Write feedback to the student that does the following:

Summarize what the student has achieved, with concrete examples from the answer that show this.
Point out areas the student can develop further. Refer to specific places in the answer where the student could have gone deeper, made connections, or generalized.

Use encouraging and constructive language. Write directly to the student ("you").
Don't refer to SOLO levels or numerical scores – phrase the feedback as academic guidance.
